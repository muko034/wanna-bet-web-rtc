# Wanna Bet Rules

## Goal
Each round, players try to predict whether the challenger will succeed at a challenge. The goal is to keep the most points by making smart bets and surviving the round sequence.

## Setup
- The game starts with at least two players.
- Each player begins with the same starting amount of points (100).
- One player is designated as the challenger for the current round.

## Round flow
1. A challenge is presented to the challenger.
2. The other players choose whether they believe the challenger will succeed or fail.
3. Each Bettor places a bet with a chosen amount and a YES/NO prediction.
4. The challenger does not place a bet.
5. After all bets are placed, the challenge is resolved.
6. The outcome is applied to all player scores.
7. The challenger rotates to the next player, and a new round begins.

## Betting rules
- Players may only bet up to half of their current points (rounded down), with a minimum bet of 1.
- A player with exactly 1 point may still bet their full 1 point, as an exception to the half-of-points cap.
- Bets are made only by Bettors.
- A bet includes:
    - amount
    - prediction: YES or NO

## Resolution rules
- The challenge is judged as either successful (YES) or unsuccessful (NO).
- Payouts are independent per player, not drawn from a shared pool — the game is **not** zero-sum; points can be created or removed from the game entirely.
- If the outcome is YES:
    - Players who predicted YES gain their bet amount.
    - Players who predicted NO lose their bet amount.
    - The challenger gains the sum of what the NO-predicting players lost.
- If the outcome is NO:
    - Players who predicted NO gain their bet amount.
    - Players who predicted YES lose their bet amount.
    - The challenger's points do not change.
- No player's points can ever drop below 1 as a result of a bet.

## Winning and losing
- Players keep playing until the game is ended.
- The winner is the player with the highest point total when the game ends.
- A player can lose points if they make an incorrect prediction; the challenger never loses points directly from a round's resolution, only bettors do.

## Player status
- The host may pause a player, skipping them from betting and rotation until resumed, or remove a player from the game entirely.
- A paused player is not waited on: the round resolves without their bet, and a bet they already placed stays. If the
  paused player is the challenger, the round is discarded with no points change and the next unpaused player becomes the challenger.
- The host resumes a player they paused. The player is eligible at once, even in the middle of a round, so a round that
  was ready to be judged goes back to waiting for their bet. A player who sat out is not the host's to resume: the host
  has no resume for them, and only the player returning ends it.
- A host pause outranks a sit out: the paused player cannot undo it by stepping away and returning.
- The host removes a player from the game for good, a player who sat out included, after confirming by name. The
  player's open bet is dropped with no points change, and if they were the challenger the round is discarded and the
  next unpaused player becomes the challenger. A removed player is not ranked on the leaderboard or the game result screen.
  Removing the last guest ends the room.
- The host can also remove a guest in the lobby, before the game starts, after confirming by name. Nobody else is told: the
  guest simply leaves the roster. The removed guest may join again as a new player.
- Once the game started, someone with the invite link can ask to join. The host sees the request and admits or
  declines it; in the lobby nobody asks, guests enter at once. The host handles requests one at a time, in arrival
  order, and a request disappears if the guest cancels or closes the page before the host answers.
- An admitted player (a late joiner) is an active bettor at once, even in an open round, and is last in the challenger
  rotation. The current challenger and round stay, and a host who was ready to judge goes back to waiting for the new
  bettor's bet. The late joiner starts with the points the host sets; by default the lowest points among active
  players, the host included. A name that matches a player in the game gets a "(n)" suffix.
- A declined guest may ask again. A guest who left or was removed asks the same way and returns as a new player with
  new points; the old row stays removed.
- The room holds at most 20 players, the host included. A request that arrives when the room is full is refused, and
  the cap is checked again when the host admits.
- There is no automatic skipping for a disconnected player — a round waits for them unless the host pauses or removes them.

## Game ending
- The game ends when the host or the group decides the session is over.
- The session may also end when no further rounds are desired or the game is manually stopped.

## A good task

A good task is one that is:
- Clear and binary: success/failure is obvious to everyone.
- Short and repeatable: easy to judge in a few seconds or under a minute.
- Observable: everyone can see whether the challenge was completed.
- Fair and understandable: no hidden rules, no subjective grading.
- Socially fun: simple, playful, and slightly risky without being dangerous.
- Balanced: strong enough to create tension, but not so hard that most bets are pointless.
- Low ambiguity: “did they complete it?” should be answerable without arguments.
